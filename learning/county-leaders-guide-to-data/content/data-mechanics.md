You do not need to be a software engineer to lead a data-driven county, but you do need a shared vocabulary of how data moves.

- **How Information is Structured:** The goal is to move from *unstructured* information (like a scanned PDF of a permit application) to *structured* data (a digital portal with dropdown menus). Structured data can be counted and mapped; unstructured data must be read by a human.
- **How We Interface with Data:**
    - *Transactional Systems:* The software your frontline staff uses to input facts (e.g., dispatch systems, permitting software, point-of-sale systems).
    - *Analytical Systems (Dashboards):* Visual tools like charts and maps used by leadership to see the big picture in real-time, pulling facts from the transactional systems.
- **Data Hygiene ("Garbage In, Garbage Out"):** Data hygiene is the discipline of ensuring information is accurate, complete, and standard across the county (e.g., deciding whether everyone uses "Main Street," "Main St.", or "Mn St."). Poor hygiene isn't just an inefficiency—it leads to bad policy decisions and represents a massive legal and privacy liability if sensitive records are mismanaged or duplicated.
