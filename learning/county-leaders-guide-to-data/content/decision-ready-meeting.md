The next significant budget or service decision is an opportunity to change the conversation. Choose one decision on an upcoming agenda. Before discussing a recommendation, ask the responsible manager to bring a short, structured account of the evidence.

## Five questions to put on the agenda

1. **What decision are we making?** Name the service problem, proposed action, and who will be affected.
2. **What facts describe the need?** Ask for counts, trends, and the period measured, not only an anecdote.
3. **How reliable are those facts?** Identify missing entries, inconsistent definitions, and assumptions.
4. **What alternatives were considered?** Compare the request with changes to current practices or resources.
5. **What will we measure afterward?** Choose an outcome that will indicate whether the decision worked.

If the evidence includes individual-level or sensitive records, ask for an appropriate aggregate or otherwise authorized view. The goal is a better decision, not unrestricted circulation of sensitive information.
