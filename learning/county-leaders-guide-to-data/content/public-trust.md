The greatest barrier to becoming a data-smart county is not technical; it is territorial. It is common to hear department heads talk about "my data" or "the Sheriff's data" versus "Public Health's data."

To move forward, leadership must establish a fundamental paradigm shift: **Government agencies do not own data. The constituents do.**

County government data is a public asset, generated by and belonging to the taxpayers. The county is merely a **steward** of that asset. A steward has two strict responsibilities:
1. **Extract Maximum Value:** Use the information to improve services, increase efficiency, and save tax dollars.
2. **Protect the Vulnerable:** Fiercely defend the privacy, security, and integrity of the personal, criminal, or sensitive health elements within that information.

When data is understood as a public trust, departmental silos lose their justification. No single agency can hoard information or refuse to share it with other county departments under the guise of "ownership." You are co-stewards of the public's information, and sharing it securely across operational lines is a requirement of public service, not a favor.
