You do not have to become a database expert. You can make schema questions normal in meetings.

Ask these seven questions when a project depends on information moving between systems:

1. **Is there a documented, stable schema?** Can staff explain it in ordinary language?
2. **Do the fields mean the same thing?** Where do definitions differ?
3. **How do records match?** Which identifiers carry through the handoff?
4. **Does it fit our other application?** Has someone demonstrated the mapping with a named system?
5. **Who manages change?** Who documents, tests, and communicates new fields and values?
6. **Is the exchange appropriate?** Which data is necessary, authorized, and protected?
7. **Can we take our information with us?** Are exports and field definitions available if a vendor changes?

You are not asking the team to recite code. You are asking it to show a dependable agreement.

## If you only remember two sentences

> Does this have a stable schema?

> Does this integrate well with the schema of our other application?

Follow with **"Can someone show us a real example?"**

A good answer may uncover work that still needs doing. That is progress, not failure. Discovering the mismatch before a rollout is cheaper than explaining it to the Board after inaccurate numbers are published.
