A technology team can tell you where a field lives. It cannot decide alone what **Successfully Referred**, **Case Closed**, or **Service Delivered** should mean for a program.

Those are business decisions. Program staff understand the work, legal and privacy specialists understand constraints, and technology staff understand how systems represent and exchange information. Schema governance needs all of them.

That does **not** mean every County application must use one enormous, identical schema. Departments have different missions. The goal is consistency where information needs to connect—and honest, documented differences where it does not.

A lightweight shared definition should answer:

- What is this field supposed to describe?
- What values may appear in it?
- Who is responsible for deciding when the meaning changes?
- Which applications or reports rely on it?

When nobody can name the person or group responsible for a definition, disagreements tend to persist until they become expensive integration problems.

**Leadership question:** *Who is the business steward for these definitions, and who approves changes that affect other departments?*

Departments do not have to agree on everything. They do need an agreed way to resolve the definitions that affect shared work.
