If two systems can understand each other's data, that does **not** mean every field should move between them.

The schema describes what information exists and how it is organized. It does not grant permission to disclose protected information. A transfer still needs an approved purpose, appropriate legal and policy review, access controls, and limits on what is necessary.

Consider a referral handoff. The receiving program may need to know that a referral was accepted, declined, or still pending. It may not need the complete case history that produced that status.

A good integration design asks which facts the receiving team actually needs. It should not default to copying the entire record simply because that is technically easier.

## The right paired questions

- **Can these schemas communicate?** That addresses shared meaning and dependable exchange.
- **Should these facts be shared?** That addresses purpose, privacy, security, authorization, and retention.

The answer to the first can be yes while the answer to the second is no—or yes only for a limited set of fields.

**Leadership question:** *Which fields are necessary for this handoff, and who has approved access to them?*

Schema compatibility and good stewardship reinforce each other. Neither replaces the other.
