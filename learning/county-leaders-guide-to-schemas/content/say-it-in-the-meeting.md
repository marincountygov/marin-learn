Schema discussions should feel as ordinary as asking for the budget, timeline, or staffing plan.

| Instead of asking... | Ask... |
| --- | --- |
| "Does the product integrate?" | "Can you show how its fields and statuses match our existing application?" |
| "Can we get a spreadsheet?" | "Will the export include understandable, documented definitions and stable IDs?" |
| "Will this report be accurate?" | "Are we counting the same thing in both systems?" |
| "Can IT fix that later?" | "Who owns the definitions and tests changes before the connection breaks?" |

A responsible team may answer, **"Not yet—we found a mismatch."** That is useful information. It gives leadership a real choice about cost, scope, and timing. Do not punish the team for surfacing it.

## A useful closing habit

At the end of a purchasing, reporting, or integration discussion, ask for three plain-language statements:

- **We agree what the important fields mean.**
- **We know how the two systems match records and handle changes.**
- **We know who maintains the agreement and what information is permitted to move.**

If those statements cannot yet be supported, the project may still be worthwhile. It is simply not ready to be described as a proven integration.

**The takeaway:** You do not need to speak in acronyms. You need to make "schema" a comfortable word for a very practical question: *Are we talking about the same thing?*
