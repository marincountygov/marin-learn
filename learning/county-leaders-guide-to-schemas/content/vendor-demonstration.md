During a software demonstration, "We have an API" or "We export to Excel" can sound reassuring. Those may be useful features. Neither proves that the information matches what another County application expects.

A better conversation names the other application and the County task. For example: **Show how this proposed work-order system would exchange job identifiers, status, dates, and costs with our finance system.**

Ask for a practical demonstration or documented example, not a slide declaring a product "integrated."

## Questions for a vendor or implementation team

1. Can you provide documented field definitions and allowed values?
2. What identifiers remain stable when records change?
3. Which fields map to our named existing system, and which do not?
4. How are corrections and updates handled after the first transfer?
5. How are schema changes announced, versioned, and tested?
6. Can we export our records and their definitions in a usable format if we change systems?
7. Which integration work, maintenance, and changes carry additional costs?

A vendor may reasonably need time to produce this evidence. The important thing is a clear, reviewable answer before the County depends on the connection.

**Leadership question:** *Does this integrate well with the schema of our other application? Show us how you know.*

These are evaluation questions, not a substitute for County procurement, security, or contracting requirements.
