A County does not need to replace every application to become better at schemas. Start with a real handoff that already matters.

Choose a contained example, such as a facilities work order moving from service completion to payment reporting. Include the program owner, the receiving team, technology staff, and the right privacy or security reviewers.

## A small first-month exercise

1. **Pick one decision.** What is the business question this connection should answer?
2. **Choose two systems.** Name the sending application and the receiving application.
3. **Compare a few fields.** Start with record ID, status, and one meaningful date or amount.
4. **Test sample records.** Include a normal example, a missing value, and a record that changes after initial entry. Use approved test data.
5. **Write down the mismatches.** Which meanings, identifiers, or allowed values differ?
6. **Agree on ownership.** Who maintains the translation and is notified when either schema changes?
7. **Repeat the test.** Confirm that the handoff remains understandable after a change.

Success is not "a consultant built a connection." Success is that staff can explain the information flow, spot an exception, and keep it working without depending on undocumented knowledge.

**Leadership question:** *What one cross-system handoff can we demonstrate before promising a larger integration?*
