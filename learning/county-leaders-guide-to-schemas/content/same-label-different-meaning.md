Two departments send leadership a report with a column labeled **People Served**. One department counts each person once during the year. The other counts every visit, including repeat visits by the same person.

Both reports may be internally accurate. Together, they can produce an inaccurate conclusion.

That is a schema problem: the label matches, but the definition does not. The reverse also happens. One system says **Request Number**, another says **Service ID**, and both might be referring to the same underlying request.

A spreadsheet export does not solve this. A spreadsheet moves values. It does not automatically explain what those values mean, which definitions were used, or whether a record in one system refers to the same record in another.

## A practical warning sign

When staff say, "We will clean that up in Excel," ask *why*. Are they fixing a few data-entry mistakes, or repeatedly translating the same mismatched definitions between two systems?

Repeated manual translation is not just an inconvenience. It makes reporting slower and less reliable, and it can hide problems that surface only after a budget or program decision.

**Leadership question:** *When two systems report this field, are they describing the same thing? How have we verified that?*
